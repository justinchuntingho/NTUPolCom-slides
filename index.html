Takeaways

Heterogeneous Effects of Repression

  • Non-uniform changes across actors or page types
  • Politician & political pages show clear short‑term contractions in sensitive topics
  • Business and influencer show no measurable intervention effects

Strategic Topic Avoidance

  • Repression shifts what actors discuss rather than eliminating discussion
  • Actors avoid high‑risk or politically sensitive topics
    (confrontational and non-confrontational collective actions)
  • Safer forms of expression persist. Tactical adaptation under repression?*

Abeyance Dynamics

  • Despite declining sensitive content, overall activity does not collapse post‑NSL
  • “No Relevant Category” content increases
  • Online activity preserves networks and communicative continuity
  • Dissent moves into the background, not necessarily disappearing

No Evidence of Tactical Radicalisation

  • Post‑NSL environment shows little or no tactical escalation
  • Confrontational and tactical discussions contract rather than rise
  • Violent or confrontational discourse remains low or declines further

Rethinking the role of the National Security Law

  • Are political researchers asking the wrong question?
  • Is the NSL the cause or consequences?
  • Formalises coercive dynamics already in place
  • But when did the repressive effect first take place?
